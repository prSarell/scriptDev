Follicle Rig Tool - Notes
=========================
Date: 2026-06-07

--- WHAT IT DOES ---

A rigging-shelf tool for follicle-driven secondary rigs - the classic example
being a bat wing: a NURBS or polygon surface is skinned to the rig's main
joints (the wing bones), follicles are scattered across that surface, and a
joint riding under each follicle drives secondary geometry (the wing
membrane). Chain: surface drives follicle -> follicle drives joint -> joint
drives character. Nothing fancy or expensive happens anywhere in that chain,
which is the whole point - cheap, robust secondary motion.

Built in rigDev/follicleRig_api.py (~600 lines, all logic, fully unit-tested
headlessly via mayapy) and rigDev/follicleRig_ui.py (PySide6 shelf window).

--- TOOL 1: FOLLICLE PLACEMENT (3 modes, one shared backend) ---

All three modes funnel through buildFollicleRig(), which creates one
follicle + one driven joint per (u, v) pair and names them
<base>_<NN>_FOL / <base>_<NN>_JNT.

1. NURBS Grid - evenly spaced uCount x vCount follicles across a NURBS
   surface, edge to edge, row-major.
2. Polygon Vertices - place a follicle at each selected mesh vertex's UV,
   in selection order (validates single-mesh selection and that the mesh
   is UV-mapped).
3. Curve on Surface - walk a curve's CVs in order, find each one's closest
   UV on the surface, and place a follicle there. Named from the curve
   (not the surface), so a hand-drawn "battWingPath" curve produces
   battWingPath_00_FOL, battWingPath_01_FOL, etc. Warns (without failing)
   if a CV sits too far from the surface - usually a sign the curve wasn't
   drawn live/snapped onto it.

Key lesson: closestPointOnSurface (NURBS) has no inputMatrix and works in
object space, while closestPointOnMesh (poly) has inputMatrix and works in
world space - the NURBS path needs explicit world<->object conversions
(MMatrix/MPoint). Either way, these closest-point nodes are built, queried
and deleted immediately - left connected and live they're an iterative
search that jitters near seams/poles and costs real performance every frame.

--- TOOL 2: UV-DRIVER CONTROL (the hard problem - and its solution) ---

Goal: a control whose translate repositions a follicle on its surface (drags
it across the U/V grid), feeling consistent - not racing ahead of or lagging
behind the drag - regardless of the surface's scale.

Fixed convention: control.translateX always drives parameterU,
translateY always drives parameterV, translateZ is left free as a lift-off
axis. Rotates are locked, hidden and unkeyable.

THE SNAG: a fixed translateX -> U mapping needs to be calibrated against
"how far you can drag before you hit the end of the surface" - and that
calibration has to be measured along the control's own local X axis. But a
surface's own local-axis layout is arbitrary (Maya's default nurbsPlane, for
example, lies with its surface normal along its own local X - giving it
~zero span in local X and a divide-by-zero). This isn't a world-space/scene-
orientation problem - it's intrinsic to how each surface happens to be
modelled, and varies model to model.

THE FIX (proposed by the user, who built a version of this rig 15+ years
ago): build a second, static "anchor" follicle at the exact same (u, v) as
the driven follicle, and parent the control under the anchor at local origin.

This works because of a fact about Maya follicles that isn't obvious but is
fundamental to how groomed hair stays aligned to a deforming surface: a
follicle transform's local axes are ALWAYS oriented to the surface's own
U/V grain at that point - local X = the surface's U-tangent direction,
local Y = the V-tangent direction, local Z = the surface normal. (Confirmed
empirically: built a follicle on an arbitrarily rotated surface, queried the
true tangent/normal vectors via pointOnSurfaceInfo, and the dot products
were exactly 1.000 / 0.000 / 0.000 for each axis pairing.)

So the anchor follicle hands the control a perfectly UV-aligned coordinate
frame for free - no custom tangent-vector math, no per-surface special
casing, and the control's rest position becomes exactly (0, 0, 0), which
collapses the calibration network back down to its simplest form (no
delta-from-rest subtraction needed).

Calibration itself uses a one-shot finite-difference probe
(_calibrationSpan): nudge a temporary follicle by a small epsilon in U or V,
measure the world-space distance moved, divide by epsilon to get "world
units per unit change in U/V" at that exact point, then delete the probe.
This is deliberately surface-type-agnostic - it works identically for NURBS
and polygon meshes, where a NURBS-only node like pointOnSurfaceInfo could
not have been used.

Per-axis remap network: multiplyDivide(x sensitivity) -> multiplyDivide
(/ calibration span) -> plusMinusAverage(+ starting U or V) -> clamp(0-1)
-> follicle.parameterU/V. `sensitivity` is a non-keyable, channel-box-only
rigger tuning knob - lock and hide it once tuned, before handoff to
animators.

Control shape: a flat 4-pointed arrow/cross in the XY plane (normal +Z),
matching the new axis convention.

--- TOOL 3: JOINT-DRIVER CONTROL ---

Goal: give the animator extra translate/rotate offset on the joint under
each follicle, at very low cost - "nothing fancy happening".

Solution: build a "stop sign on a pole" control (a vertical line rising
along local Z - the surface normal once parented under a follicle - capped
with an octagon; tall and large enough to be easy to see and easy to select,
built as two curve shapes under one transform so either part selects it).
Parent it as a SIBLING of the joint under the follicle, zero it to coincide
exactly with the follicle's pivot/orientation, then parent-constrain the
joint to it.

Because both control and joint share the follicle as their parent and
coincide at rest, the constraint reduces to a plain mirror of the control's
local transform onto the joint's - no compensation, no fighting, minimal
evaluation cost. All channels (translate AND rotate) stay open, keyable and
unlocked - there's no calibration math on this one at all.

--- LAUNCH BUTTON ---

Paste into the Maya Script Editor (Python tab), or wire up as a shelf button:

    import sys
    sys.path.insert(0, r'C:\Users\patsa\OneDrive\Documents\maya\scriptDev\rigDev')

    import follicleRig_ui
    follicleRig_ui.show()

If you edit the modules and want to re-launch without restarting Maya:

    import importlib
    import follicleRig_api
    import follicleRig_ui
    importlib.reload(follicleRig_api)
    importlib.reload(follicleRig_ui)
    follicleRig_ui.show()

Golden path to test (Task #8): create follicles with each of the 3 placement
modes on a real surface -> select a follicle + its surface and add a UV
driver control -> drag it and confirm the follicle moves smoothly across the
surface without racing ahead or lagging -> select a follicle + its joint and
add a joint driver control -> move/rotate it and confirm the joint follows.
