## Species 1: Corymbia citriodora (Lemon-Scented Gum)

### Trunk
- Mature height: 25-40 m, occasionally 50 m
- DBH: 60-130 cm; champion ~133 cm
- Taper: Neiloid at bottom 3-4%, paraboloid from 4-94%, conical top 6%. Form factor ~0.45-0.50
- Straight to sinuous; single-stemmed

### Branching
- Primary branch angle: ~30-50 degrees from vertical (ascending)
- First branch height: ~67% of total height
- Few ascending scaffold branches, sparse/open
- Branchlets and foliage pendulous at tips (90+ degrees)

### Crown
- Shape: Symmetrical, open, rounded to oval
- Crown width: 0.3-0.5x height (cultivated), 0.6-0.7x (open-grown)
- Crown depth: ~33-40% of total height
- Sparse, open canopy

### Growth Stages
- Sapling (0-5 yr): 2.5-3.7 m/yr growth. Lignotuber develops. Juvenile opposite leaves. Spongy periderm bark
- Juvenile (5-15 yr): Transition to alternate adult leaves. 20 m in 10 years. Crown lifts. Bark transitions to smooth
- Mature (15-50+ yr): 25-40 m. Crown well-lifted. Clear bole at two-thirds. Annual bark shedding established
- Old growth: Can exceed 50 m. Crown spread up to 29 m. Trunk 1.3+ m diameter

### Bark
- Completely smooth throughout — no rough bark at base
- Colors: white, cream, pink, coppery, red, blue-grey; powdery surface
- Shedding: thin curling flakes in spring/summer
- Surface texture: "evenly rippled musculature"

### Root System
- Lateral roots near surface, can extend 30 m from trunk
- 90% of root mass in top 30 cm
- No buttressing

## Species 2: Eucalyptus pauciflora (Snow Gum)

### Trunk
- Height varies by subspecies/altitude:
  - subsp. pauciflora (lowland): 20-30 m
  - subsp. niphophila (alpine treeline): typically 7 m, often under 5 m
  - subsp. debeuzevillei: 10-12 m
- DBH: maximum ~100 cm
- Characteristically crooked and twisted at higher altitudes
- Single-stemmed (lowland) or multi-stemmed from lignotuber (alpine, 2-4+ stems)

### Branching
- Gnarled, twisted branches throughout
- Very low branching in alpine forms ("from near the base")
- Higher first branch in sheltered lowland forms
- Open and spreading, pendulous foliage

### Crown
- Alpine: open, spreading, irregular
- Lowland: broad elliptical, "wine goblet" shape
- Crown width: roughly 0.3-0.7x height
- Sparse/open canopy

### Growth Stages
- Sapling: 1.0-1.5 m/yr. Lignotuber develops within first year
- Juvenile: Opposite leaves, dull bluish green
- Mature: Adult alternate leaves, thick, glossy, waxy. Full twisted form develops. Multi-colored bark mosaic
- Old growth (300+ yr): Basal girth exceeding 250 cm

### Bark
- Completely smooth throughout — no rough bark
- Defining feature: mosaic of 4-6 simultaneous colors (white, cream, grey, yellow, green, olive, pink, bronze, copper, red)
- Shedding: ribbons, strips, jigsaw-like pieces. Primary in midsummer
- Young bark: dark red, lustrous, burgundy with white bloom
- Scribbly moth trails common (fine meandering surface lines)

### Subspecies Parameters

| Subspecies | Height | Form | Elevation | Cold Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pauciflora | 20-30 m | Single-stem tree | >700 m | -8 to -14C |
| niphophila | 5-10 m | Mallee/twisted | 1750-1900 m | -14 to -23C |
| debeuzevillei | 10-12 m | Dense stunted tree | High altitude | Highly cold-tolerant |

### Distinctive Features
- Altitude/exposure is the primary parameter driving form
- Multi-stem growth from lignotuber in alpine forms
- Scribbly bark (moth larvae galleries)

## Species 3: Eucalyptus regnans (Mountain Ash)

### Trunk
- Mature height: 70-114 m. Tallest living: 100.5 m. Historical max: 114.3 m
- DBH: typical 2.5 m, largest living 6.88 m, largest known 10.77 m
- Buttress extent: 9-18 m up the trunk
- Diameter reduces 29-36% from breast height to 8 m above
- Cross-section deviates ~21% from perfect circle due to buttressing
- Very straight clear bole; single-stemmed (obligate seeder)

### Branching
- Clear bole commonly 65%+ of total height
- Upper branches more erect; lower crown branches more horizontal
- Few branches, open crown, small relative to tree size

### Crown
- Young: narrow, conical/pointy
- Pole stage (~30 m): still conical
- Spar stage: spreads out
- Mature: broadens
- Old growth: crown opening, stag-headed, dead branches, epicormic shoots on trunk
- Crown width: 9-15 m typical
- Crown depth: ~35% or less of total height

### Growth Stages
- Post-fire seedling: up to 2.5 million seedlings/hectare initially
- Sapling (0-20 yr): 1-2 m/yr, narrow conical crowns
- Pole stage (~30 m): conical crown, aggressive self-thinning
- Spar stage: crown spreads, almost full height reached
- Mature (80-100+ yr): crown broadens, density 30-40 trees/hectare
- Old growth (220+ yr): hollows begin at ~120 years
- Senescent (300-500 yr): crown opens, central dieback, stag-headed

### Bark
- Rough bark (basal stocking): 5-20 m from ground (typically 10-15 m). Fibrous/crumbly, grey/brown/black
- Smooth bark (upper): cream, grey-green, pale grey. Very thin (0.6-0.8 cm). Sheds in long ribbons
- Bark thickness reduces 75-83% from breast height to 8 m above

### Root System
- Major buttressing: 9-18 m up trunk
- Shallow: 90% in top 30 cm
- Root volume = 2.33260 x DBH-squared
- ~20 major laterals per tree

### Distinctive Features
- Tallest flowering plant on Earth
- Obligate seeder — killed by fire, no lignotuber, no resprouting
- Massive self-thinning: 2.5 million seedlings/ha to 30-40 mature trees/ha
- Hollow formation: minimum 120 years

## Species 4: Eucalyptus camaldulensis (River Red Gum)

### Trunk
- Mature height: 20-30 m typical, max 45-50 m
- DBH: typical 1.0-2.0 m, record 4.5-4.7 m
- Open-grown: short, thick, characteristically curved bole
- Often "massive twisted boles" in old specimens
- Single-stemmed typically; multi-stem from lignotuber if damaged

### Branching
- First branch height (open-grown): very low, ~2-5 m (10-25% of height)
- First branch height (forest): up to 20 m (67%)
- Heavy, twisting branches at wide angles
- Primary branches ascending to widely spreading; heavy horizontal and drooping limbs in mature trees

### Crown
- Shape: open, widely spreading, irregular
- Crown width can equal or exceed tree height (ratio 1.0-1.17)
- Crown width: 10-20 m typical, record 29 m
- Light, sparse, open canopy with pendulous leaves

### Growth Stages
- Sapling: fast vertical growth (up to 3-7 m/yr optimal). Narrow upright crown
- Mature: crown spreads, short thick bole in open-grown. Production maturity at 90-120 yr / ~50 cm DBH
- Old growth (120-180+ yr): hollow formation, crown dieback, massive spreading canopy, epicormic shooting
- Can live 500-1000 years

### Bark
- Rough bark at base: 1-2 m. Dark, reddish-brown or black, fibrous
- Smooth bark (remainder): white, cream, grey, yellow-green, pinkish-grey mottled
- Sheds in long strips revealing pale to pinkish underlayers

### Root System
- Lateral extent: up to 2.5x tree height
- Depth: up to 3 m with sinker roots
- 50% in top 10-30 cm
- Extensive exposed surface roots along waterways
- No buttressing

### Distinctive Features
- Summer limb drop — heavy lateral branches fracture in hot/humid weather
- Extensive epicormic shooting from bark strands throughout trunk/branches
- Can shed two-thirds of foliage in drought, regrowing rapidly after rain
- Longevity: 500-1000 years

## L-System and Procedural Modeling Parameters

### Architectural Model (Attims)
All four species follow Attims's model:
- Monopodial trunk (single dominant central axis)
- Orthotropic branches (morphogenetically equivalent to trunk)
- Continuous/diffuse branching (not rhythmic/seasonal)
- Lateral flowering (never terminal)

### Phyllotaxis
- Juvenile: opposite-decussate (90 degree divergence per pair)
- Adult: alternate, ~137.5 degrees (golden angle)

### Leaf Inclination
- Predominantly erectophile (70-80 degrees from horizontal)
- 41 of 71 measured eucalyptus species erectophile, 15 spherical, 13 uniform

### Branch Scaling (Da Vinci's Rule)
- Scaling exponent ~2.0 (area-preserving)
- Sub-tree scaling (4 species, 19 saplings):
  - Length vs Diameter slope: 2.06 (R-sq 0.842)
  - Surface Area vs Volume slope: 0.798 (R-sq 0.989)
  - Diameter vs Volume slope: 0.314 (R-sq 0.928)

### Stem Taper (from E. grandis / E. dunnii)
- Neiloid-to-paraboloid transition: 3.1-3.7% of height
- Paraboloid-to-cone transition: 86.8-94.1% of height

### Weber-Penn Adaptation

| Parameter | Suggested Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| BaseSize | 0.50-0.75 | High clear bole |
| Shape | 4 or 7 | Conical to open/irregular |
| Scale | 13-55 | Species-dependent |
| Level 1 DownAngle | 50-60 | Ascending primary branches |
| Level 1 DownAngleV | -40 to -50 | Age-dependent droop |
| Level 1 Branches | 30-40 | Sparser than deciduous |
| Level 1 CurveV | 80-120 | Irregular/drooping character |
| AttractionUp | 0.3-0.5 | Moderate phototropism |
| Leaves | 20-30 | Sparse canopy |

### Eucalyptus-Specific Growth Rules
1. Epicormic sprouting: dense irregular sprouting along full trunk/branch length after fire/damage
2. Lignotuber regrowth: multi-stem "mallee" form from basal woody swelling
3. Strong self-pruning: long clear bole, lower branches die and shed
4. Continuous growth: branching points distributed evenly (not at annual nodes)
5. Bark type by height: rough at base transitioning to smooth above (species-dependent)

## Species 5 (candidate): Eucalyptus caesia (Silver Princess Gum)

Not yet modeled as its own species in `eucalyptusGen.py` — flagged for addition. Discovered by accident: running the existing **citriodora** parameters at a much smaller scale produced a tree that reads convincingly as a Silver Princess Gum.

**Working preset (citriodora params, until caesia gets its own SPECIES entry):**
- species = `citriodora`, age = `mature`, density = `typical`
- seed = `9803`
- scale = `0.095`

**Why this works:** *E. caesia* is a small mallee-form gum (~3-9 m, weeping pendulous branches, silvery bark) — nowhere near citriodora's real 25-40 m straight-trunked form. Scaling citriodora down to ~0.095 happens to land in caesia's real height range (citriodora's 25-40 m base range x 0.095 ≈ 2.4-3.8 m), so the "look" is really just citriodora's proportions correctly re-sized, not a true caesia model.

**If/when this becomes a real `SPECIES['caesia']` entry, it needs its own research pass, not just a scaled citriodora:**
- Real height/DBH range for a mallee-form gum (~3-9 m typical, multi-stemmed from a lignotuber like pauciflora, not single-trunked like citriodora)
- Actual weeping branch character (caesia is known for pendulous, drooping branches — closer to camaldulensis's droop than citriodora's now-upright V-branching, since citriodora was recently tuned to be *less* droopy)
- Silvery/glaucous bark and foliage color, distinct from citriodora's white/cream/pink/copper
- Large solitary pendulous pink/red flowers and bell-shaped fruit (distinctive of caesia, not represented by any current species)
- TreeQSM: reconstructs 95% of branches >30cm with 99% correct branching order
